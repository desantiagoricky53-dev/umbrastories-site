The Isdal Woman

Isdalen, Bergen, Norway · November 1970

Date
November 29, 1970
Location
Isdalen, Bergen, Norway
Status
Never Identified
Aliases
8 False Identities

On November 29, 1970, a man and his two daughters hiking in the Isdalen valley, in the hills above Bergen, Norway, came upon the burned body of a woman. Identifying labels had been removed from her belongings. Three days later, police traced two suitcases at Bergen railway station to her: inside were wigs, cash in several currencies, cosmetics, and a coded notepad — later deciphered as a record of dates and places she had traveled — but no passport was ever recovered. Investigators found she had moved through Norway under at least eight false identities, and witnesses described her speaking German, Flemish, and broken English. An autopsy attributed her death to a combination of sleeping pills and carbon monoxide poisoning, and police closed the case as a likely suicide. She was never identified. The case drew renewed investigation beginning in 2016; isotope analysis suggested she was born around 1930, possibly in the region of Nuremberg, Germany. She was buried in Bergen in February 1971.

The Isdal Woman case remains officially unresolved. The woman was never identified, and no person was ever charged. Police ruled her death a likely suicide, a conclusion that has been questioned ever since.
